Import part list

Paste the order's part table (tab- or space-separated). Columns expected: Part #, Name, Qty, L(inch), L(mm), W(inch), W(mm). Section header rows and the column header are detected automatically. Qty 0 rows are skipped. mm columns are used as the working dimensions.

Match an order to a recipe

Paste a production order's part list. It's fingerprinted by the multiset of part sizes + thickness and compared against saved recipes. A match means an existing stack plan applies.

How it works

Placing parts

Click a part in the left tray to arm it, press R to set its rotation, then click the pallet to drop it on the active layer. Drag placed parts to move them; they snap to the footprint walls and to each other (set a Gap to leave kerf between parts, or turn Snap off for free movement). Parts are held inside the footprint while Keep in bounds is on. Two parts overlapping within one layer turn red; overlaps between layers are fine — that's the stack. Suggest layer densely packs the current (or a new) layer with as many tray parts as fit, trying both orientations and backfilling leftover space, then leaves the rest in the tray — click it again to fill the next layer. A starting point you can rearrange.

Auto-stacking & cartons

Suggest layer fills one layer at a time. Build stack runs a nesting-style search (Fast / Balanced / Thorough — slower = more thorough) over many part orderings and carton placements, and hands back the best 3 distinct layouts to click between. It puts big panels low, saves small parts for the top, and reserves frame-carton voids across the top layers with panels packed around them — you set the cartons into those voids by hand. Define a carton by name and L×W×H; a 100mm carton spans 6 of the 19mm layers. The same SKU at the same effort reproduces the same result.

On a phone or tablet

Pinch to zoom, drag one finger on empty space to pan, tap Fit to reset the view. Tap a part to select it — the bar at the bottom nudges, rotates, duplicates, or deletes it. Use the tabs under the canvas to reach Parts, Selection, Layers, Cartons, and Settings.

Coordinate model

Coordinates are distances from the chosen origin corner of the chosen datum (footprint or pallet), measured inward, so values are always positive. X runs along the long side, Y along the short side. Angle is the part rotation in degrees. Export carries the reference point you pick plus the center, angle, and L×W, so it can be re-mapped to whatever Edge's cell expects.
